# 04 — How to Compare Three Quotes Fairly

Three quotes, apples to apples. Not five tee shots and a prayer — three real,
comparable numbers that you trust. The static worksheet in `site/worksheet.html`
does the math for you; this chapter explains why the rules are what they are.

## Rule 1 — Same coverages, or the numbers are fiction

The most common way shopping goes wrong: one quote is `50/100/50` with $500
deductibles and the next is `100/300/100` with $1,000 deductibles, and someone
chases the lowest premium. You'd be comparing a Honda Civic to a Ford F-150.

Before getting quote #2, freeze four things:

- **Liability limits** (e.g., 100/300/100)
- **UM/UIM limits** (match liability — set, then leave)
- **Deductibles** (collision and comprehensive)
- **The car, annual miles, and usage class** (pleasure vs. commute)

Write them in the "Fixed version" row of the worksheet. Every quote gets priced
against that *exact* version.

## Rule 2 — Compare the term, not the month

One quote shows $84/month over 6 months; another shows $71/month over 12.
Annual totals: $504 vs $852. But per-month, the 12-month policy is cheaper *and*
locks the rate. Always normalize to an annual cost. The worksheet does this.

## Rule 3 — Compare the company, not just the number

A rock-bottom premium from a carrier with an A.M. Best rating of "C" and a
horror-show complaint record will cost you years of frustration at claim time.
Three public, free things to check (5 minutes total):

1. **AM Best / Demotech financial strength** — carrier's own site or AM Best's
   free lookup.
2. **NAIC complaint index** — natl consumer database; anything much over 1.0
   (relative to size) deserves a question.
3. **J.D. Power claims satisfaction** (or state DOI market conduct reports).

None of this requires handing over personal data. Just carrier names.

## Rule 4 — Check the fine print mods

Quotes often differ in these easy-to-miss riders:

- Original-equipment-manufacturer (OEM) parts requirement vs. aftermarket
- New-car replacement vs. standard ACV (actual cash value)
- Incident forgiveness vs. surcharge-on-first-incident
- Whether the quote includes rental, roadside, gap — or whether those are extra

If one quote is $90 cheaper but excludes rental and roadside, add those back
priced separately. The worksheet has a row for "optional coverages included."

## Rule 5 — One real agent, one direct carrier, one comparison site

Shopping becomes fair and fast when the three quotes come from genuinely
different channels:

1. **A captive agent** (State Farm, Allstate, GEICO direct) — one company's
   best real price.
2. **An independent agent or broker** — one conversation, five carriers behind
   it, and a human you can call in September.
3. **One additional direct carrier or a reputable comparison site** — biggest
   national, to break any local tie.

Comparison sites are fine as *triage* (they narrow candidates), but finish with
a real quote from the finalists. Never put your real SSN or driver's license
number into a site you haven't vetted — chapter 06.

## Rule 6 — Get the quote *after* you have credit-signal questions answered

Where legal, insurance credit scores move prices; where illegal, they don't.
Whichever your state does, a soft-check quote that turns into a "will verify
at bind" surprise is the most common gotcha. Simply ask: "Will this quote
change if you check my credit / CLUE / driving record at bind time?" If yes,
get the *conditional* price before you commit time to the comparison.

## Rule 7 — Use the worksheet, not memory

Fill in `site/worksheet.html` as each quote arrives. Do not trust:

- the rep's memory of "we usually beat that"
- your own recall three days later
- screenshots of different-format numbers

The worksheet normalizes everything to annual cost with identical coverages and
shows a delta column. Fifteen minutes prep on chapter 04's worksheet page saves
an hour of dread.

## The callback move

Once the worksheet shows quote #1 clearly cheapest:

1. Call your *current* insurer with the competing quote in hand (actual numbers
   from the worksheet — never "they said they'd beat you," which sounds fake
   because it is).
2. Ask: "Can you match or beat this at the same coverage version?" Many carriers
   have a retention budget and will use it once.
3. If they can't — that's a real answer to take calmly. Loyalty discount math
   rarely makes up a $300/year gap.
4. **Never cancel the old policy before the new one is bound.** The single
   nastiest self-inflicted wound in this whole game is a one-day lapse, which
   raises your future rates and puts you driving uninsured.

## Timeline that fits in a week

- **Day 1** — Freeze coverage version, start worksheet (20 min).
- **Day 2** — Quote from a direct carrier (15 min).
- **Day 3** — Call an independent agent (15 min).
- **Day 4** — Third quote from another channel (15 min).
- **Day 5** — Read the dec pages, check complaint indexes (30 min).
- **Day 6** — Callback move on current insurer; bind the winner (20 min).
- **Day 7** — Calendar the next 6-month shopping reminder (5 min).

Total: under 3 hours, spread across a week. Zero dread required.
