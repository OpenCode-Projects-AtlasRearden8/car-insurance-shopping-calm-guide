# 07 — Checklists and Calm Call Scripts

Everything before this was learning. This chapter is doing. Short checklists,
one page of scripts, and the two rules that prevent 90% of disasters.

## The two ironclad rules

1. **Never let a policy lapse — not even one day.** Overlapping coverage is
   fine; a gap raises future rates and makes you legally uninsured.
2. **A quote is not a commitment, and "I'm shopping" is a complete sentence.**
   Use it verbatim whenever anyone pushes.

## The 10-minute pre-call checklist

Bring to every call (or have open in a note):

- [ ] Exactly your coverage version (chapter 04), written down: limits and
      deductibles on one line.
- [ ] Vehicle basics: year, make, model, trim, roughly annual miles, usage
      (pleasure/commute), primary garaging ZIP.
- [ ] Average of "I'd pay tomorrow without borrowing" — your real deductible
      ceiling.
- [ ] Your ACV estimate (chapter 05) if the car is 8+ years old.
- [ ] The quote-tracking worksheet open in your browser.
- [ ] Placeholder facts ready — no personal data needed on *your* side of the
      notes (they will collect their own application data through secure
      channels; that part's fine — it's *chat logs* and *screenshots* you keep
      clean).
- [ ] A one-line bottom boundary: "At or under $______ for this version."

## The scripts

### Opening a call (any channel)

> "Hi — I'm comparing rates before my renewal. Could I get a quote for a
> specific coverage version I have written down? Same coverages for everyone."

This is polite, short, and signals comparison-shopping instantly.

### Holding your version when they offer a lowball

> "Thanks — that's a lower version than I want [limits mismatch]. Could I get
> the same version priced at 100/300/100 with a $500 deductible? Include UM/UIM
> to match, plus rental and roadside."

The magic is that you *named the version*. Agents respect it and often drop
their sales script entirely.

### Asking about discounts without dread

> "What discounts am I already getting on this quote, and is there anything
> I'd qualify for with one small change — a defensive driving course,
> paid-in-full, an affinity program for nurses?"

See chapter 03 for the full seven-question set.

### The retention call to your current insurer

> "I've been with you [N] years. I have a competing quote for the identical
> coverage at $[X] per year. Could you match or beat it? I'd genuinely like to
> stay if the number works."

Bring the actual numbers from the worksheet. A specific number makes the
retention budget real; "someone said they'd beat you" is simply ignored.

### When they push for a decision "today only"

> "I understand the rate is what it is. I shop every 6 months; if it's still
> the best version then, I'll be back. Please send the quote in writing."

No carrier with a real rate ever needs today-only from a calm customer.
Today-only is a discount-code word for pressure; treat it as data.

### Cancelling the old policy (only after the new one binds)

> "Confirming cancellation effective [date = after new bind]. I want written
> confirmation that there's no lapse and a refund of prorated premium. My
> address for documents is [whatever's already on file]."

## The validation checklist — before you pay anyone

- [ ] Dec page or written quote in hand, showing *exactly* the version you
      asked for.
- [ ] Colors checked: carrier name is the real carrier, not a "general agency"
      sub-brand.
- [ ] AM Best rating and NAIC complaint index looked up (5 min, chapter 04).
- [ ] The *term* (6 vs 12 months) and total annual cost are clear.
- [ ] Discounts listed on the dec page match what the agent promised.
- [ ] No lapse: new policy binds before the old one ends (ideally same-day
      overlap).
- [ ] Worksheet updated with the final number.

## After you switch

- [ ] Calendar reminder to re-shop in 6 months (chapter 04's Day 7).
- [ ] Update any automatic-payment references.
- [ ] Put the new insurance card in the glovebox (or the phone wallet).
- [ ] File the old dec page with the rest of your documents (keep at least a
      year of old dec pages for reference).

## If something goes wrong

- Rate jumps at bind vs. the written quote → ask for the *written* quote and
  the reason; if they can't reconcile, walk.
- Wrong coverage on the dec page → call within days; misprints are fixable and
  common.
- A claim later gets denied → that's what `insurance-appeals-calm-guide` is
  for. Every state's insurance department has a consumer complaint division —
  free, and surprisingly effective.
