# Glossary — Car Insurance, Alphabetized

Quick reference. Each entry: what it is, then one line of "why it matters when
you're shopping."

**ACV (Actual Cash Value)** — What your car would sell for today, used for
total-loss payouts. Not what you owe on the loan, not what you paid. The key
input for chapter 05's drop-full-coverage decision.

**Agent (captive)** — Sells one carrier's products only. One company, one real
price. One of chapter 04's three shopping channels.

**Agent (independent)** — Sells several carriers at once. One conversation,
multiple quotes behind it.

**Agreed value / stated value** — An alternative payout basis some policies
offer (rare for standard consumer auto). If a claim involves the "comp" version
of value rather than plain ACV, ask which applies to your policy.

**Binder** — Temporary proof of insurance issued before the formal policy is
finalized. Valid for driving; used to avoid lapses at switch time.

**Bodily injury (BI) liability** — Pays other people's injury costs when
you're at fault. Two limits: per person / per accident.

**Cancellation vs. non-renewal** — Cancellation is you or them ending
mid-term; non-renewal is a policy simply ending at term. Not renewing you
carries lower stigma than cancelling you.

**CLUE report** — LexisNexis's claims-history database. Insurers read it when
you quote; you can get your own copy once a year free.

**Collision** — Pays for your car's damage from hitting (or being hit by)
something. Carries a deductible.

**Comprehensive** — Pays for your car's damage from non-crash events (theft,
weather, animal strikes, glass). Carries a deductible.

**Comp-only** — Keeping comprehensive while dropping collision. A common,
often-sensible middle for older cars (chapter 05).

**Coverage version** — The exact bundle of limits and deductibles you want
quoted. Frozen before comparing (chapter 04, rule 1).

**Declarations page (dec page)** — The short summary sent each renewal listing
the insured, vehicles, coverages, limits, premiums, discounts. Your audit sheet.

**Deductible** — The amount you pay per claim before the carrier pays.
Collision and comprehensive each have their own.

**Discount tiers** — Carrier-specific price reductions (multi-policy, paid-in-
full, defensive driving, affinity programs). Only real after chapter 04's
apples-to-apples comparison.

**Endorsement / rider** — A policy add-on that changes coverage (gap, custom
parts, rideshare gap, new-car replacement).

**Exclusions** — Things simply not covered. Read once, even if skimming.

**Full coverage** — Informal shorthand: liability + collision + comprehensive.
Not a real product.

**Gap insurance** — Pays the loan balance above ACV after a total loss.
Essential while you owe more than the car is worth; drop when that flips.

**Hard check vs. soft check credit pull** — Insurance quotes use soft checks
(no credit-score damage), but some carriers verify harder at bind time. Ask
(chapter 04, rule 6).

**Liability** — The legal-responsibility coverage that pays others when you
cause damage/injury. Written like `50/100/50`, `100/300/100`.

**Limits** — Max payout for any coverage. "Raising limits" = buying more
payout room, usually liability first.

**MedPay (Medical Payments)** — Pays medical bills for you/passengers
regardless of fault. Smaller/private state version of PIP.

**NaIC (National Association of Insurance Commissioners)** — The association
whose database lets you look up complaint ratios per carrier. Free, five
minutes, chapter 04.

**No-fault state** — A state where your own PIP pays first for injuries,
little regard for fault. Changes how injury coverage matters.

**OEM parts endorsement** — Requires the carrier's claim shop to use
original-manufacturer parts rather than aftermarket ones. Difference matters
for newer cars.

**Payment lapse** — Even one day without coverage. A future rate-warning and a
legal hazard. Never allow it (chapter 07, rule 1).

**PIP (Personal Injury Protection)** — Medical + wage-loss coverage that pays
first in no-fault states regardless of fault.

**Policy term** — How long the policy runs: 6 or 12 months common. Compare
quotes in annual cost, not monthly (chapter 04, rule 2).

**Premium** — What you pay for the coverage itself, monthly or per term.

**Primary vs. excess coverage** — Which policy pays first (primary) versus
which tops up the rest (excess). Rideshare platform coverage is typically
excess; your personal policy is primary. The gap between the two is period 1
(see rideshare notes in chapter 03).

**Quote** — A price estimate for a coverage version. Non-binding until bound.

**Rate lock** — Some carriers guarantee the premium for the term. Ask if the
quote is lockable.

**Residual** — What remains after a total-loss check clears the loan: either a
down payment for your next car or money still owed. Ask which your gap covers.

**Soft check** — See CLUE and hard/soft pull. Your shopping rate isn't hurt by
soft pulls.

**State minimum** — The legally required floor for liability (some states add
PIP/UM floors). "Legal" and "sensible" are different (chapter 02).

**Subrogation** — When your carrier pays you, then chases the at-fault driver's
carrier to get money back (you get your deductible back if they win).

**Summons/judgment exposure** — The amount above your liability limit that a
successful lawsuit can reach: savings, home, wages. Why chapter 02 exists.

**Telematics** — The app/plug-in that scores your driving for a discount or
penalty. Read the terms before signing up for *any* telematics program.

**TERM normalization** — Chapter 04's rule: convert every quote to an annual
cost so different term lengths compare fairly.

**Umbrella policy** — Extra liability coverage on top of auto/home policies,
often ~$150–$300/year for $1M. Ask an agent, especially if you own assets.

**Underinsured motorist (UIM)** — Pays your costs when the at-fault driver has
some-but-not-enough coverage.

**Uninsured motorist (UM)** — Pays your costs when the at-fault driver has no
coverage. Best-value coverage money buys in most states.

**Usage class** — Pleasure vs. commute vs. business vs. rideshare. Rating
input; honesty is cheap, omission is a claim-denial risk.
