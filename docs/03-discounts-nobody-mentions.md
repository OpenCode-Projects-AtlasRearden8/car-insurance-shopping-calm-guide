# 03 — Discounts Nobody Mentions

Everyone knows "good driver" and "bundle the renter's policy." This chapter is
for the quiet discounts that keep showing up on real quotes because nobody
thought to ask. None of these are guaranteed — they vary by state and carrier —
but each costs one question to check.

## The quiet handful

1. **Paid-in-full discount** — paying the whole 6–12 month term at once commonly
   saves 5–10%. If paying upfront is painful, ask whether the carrier has a
   tiny "installment-fee" difference instead of a true discount; either way,
   knowing beats guessing.
2. **Paperless + paper-free billing** — under $3/month saved on some policies.
3. **Homeowner discount (even if you don't bundle)** — someone with a mortgage
   looks stable; some carriers price for it even on a car-only policy.
4. **Membership and employer groups** — Costco, AAA, credit-union affiliations,
   some unions and alumni associations. Ask the carrier to run "affinity
   groups."
5. **Defensive driving / driver improvement course** — often a real discount in
   many states (and after a violation, sometimes a point reduction). Watch for
   state-approved course lists.
6. **Low-mileage / work-from-home** — annual miles are a giant rating input.
   If your commute changed, tell them. Some carriers have pay-per-mile programs
   (Metromile-style) worth a quote if you drive under ~8k/year.
7. **Telematics programs** — the "plug-in or app" programs (Snapshot-style).
   They price your *real* driving. Do not buy blindly: ask what happens if the
   score is bad (some carriers raise rates; many now say they won't). One
   unexpected consequence people forget: a disproportionate share of brand-new
   parents and night-shift workers score poorly. Read the terms.
8. **Occupation and degree-based programs** — nurse, teacher, engineer,
   military-veteran alumni programs. Several carriers have them. Ask.
9. **Anti-theft equipment** — factory alarms sometimes qualify; aftermarket
   trackers or kill switches sometimes add more. Low value, but free to ask.
10. **New-car discounts** — airbags, anti-lock brakes, daytime running lights:
    often automatic, occasionally not applied until you call.
11. **Loyalty retention credits** — usually for renewal-time customers who
    threaten to leave (which is why chapter 04 gives you competing real quotes
    *before* you call your current insurer).
12. **Student-tier discounts** — good student (B average), away-at-school
    (car stays home), driver-training completion.
13. **Multi-device / first-responder / RN affinity programs** — if Dylan's
    nurse network sees this: several carriers run healthcare-worker and
    first-responder affinity pricing. One question each, at renewal.
14. **Annual policy instead of 6-month** — some carriers offer 12-month rate
    locks. Not always cheaper, but protects against mid-year surges.
15. **Usage-class honesty** — if you only drive for errands and groceries,
    "pleasure use" rates apply. If you moved to a safer garage (street parking
    → garage), say so. Rating inputs go stale and stale inputs go the wrong way.

## Rideshare and delivery notes

If you drive for Uber/Lyft/DoorDash with a personal policy and tell nobody, the
carrier can *deny the entire claim* during business-use periods. Fix:

- **Period 1 gap coverage riders** from most major carriers (small add-on), or
- a commercial policy if you do this full-time, or
- the platform's own (very thin) contingent coverage.

It is the single most-expensive-to-be-wrong-about line in this chapter.

## The "yes, but" checklist

Before assuming a discount applies:

- [ ] Is it allowed in my state? (Some, like credit-based or gender-based
      pricing, are banned in several states.)
- [ ] Does it apply to the *whole policy* or one coverage?
- [ ] Does it survive renewal, or is it a first-term teaser?
- [ ] Would losing it hurt more than the discount gained? (e.g., some
      telematics programs penalize hard-braking.)

## Seven questions to ask any agent

1. "What discounts am I *already* getting on this quote?" (so you can verify it
   against the dec page later)
2. "Is there anything I'd qualify for if I changed one small thing?" (defensive
   driving, paperless, paid-in-full)
3. "Do you have an affinity program for [nurses / teachers / veterans / credit
   union members]?"
4. "If I raised my deductible, how much would the premium drop?"
5. "What happens to my rate if I use the telematics program and score poorly?"
6. "Is this quote a 6-month or 12-month rate?"
7. "Are you licensed to write in my state, and is anything on this quote
   different in your home state?" (important with multi-state agents)

## What discounts *aren't*

Discounts are **not** free money. The carrier prices base rates to make the
final number whatever it needs to be — a wide-sticker-price discount is worthless
if the insurance underneath is thin. Always finish with chapter 04's apples-to-
apples coverage comparison; never compare discounts across cheap policies.
