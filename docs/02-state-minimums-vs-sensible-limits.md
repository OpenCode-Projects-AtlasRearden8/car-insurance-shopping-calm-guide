# 02 — State Minimums vs. Sensible Limits

State minimums are the floor the law sets. Nobody is asking you to live on the
floor. This chapter separates "legal" from "sensible" without panic.

## The uncomfortable math

The most common minimum bundle in the US is **`25/50/25`**:

| Real event | Typical cost | Minimum limit | You owe after limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hospital stay + rehab, one person | $100k–$500k+ | $50k | Everything above $50k |
| Multi-car highway crash, 3 vehicles | $400k+ total | $100k BI / $25k PD | The difference |

Property damage is the sneakiest one: $25,000 "property" sounds huge until the
other vehicle is a $90k truck or you slide into someone's storefront. Minimum PD
limits are commonly considered too low by almost everyone who actually prices
claims.

**The general pattern:** minimum-coverage policies are cheap every month until
one bad day, then lawsuits arrive. "Sensible" usually means raising liability
first — it tends to cost far less per dollar of added protection than any other
coverage.

## What "sensible" commonly looks like

Not advice — a hint of where conversations usually land, to discuss with a
licensed agent:

| Coverage | Typical minimum | Common sensible range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bodily injury liability | $25k/$50k | $100k/$300k |
| Property damage liability | $25k | $50k–$100k |
| Uninsured motorist | often $0 (or low) | match liability |
| Collision deductible | n/a | $500–$1,000 (what you could really pay) |
| Comprehensive deductible | n/a | $500 (glass-friendly) |
| Rental reimbursement | often $0 | $40/day, $1,200 total |
| MedPay/PIP | state-specific | follow state norms; ask |

## Suggested ladder of upgrades (cheap → meaningful)

1. **UM/UIM to match liability** — protects your family from everyone else's
   minimum policy. Often under $40 a year.
2. **Raise PD from minimum to $50k–$100k** — noticeable but modest cost.
3. **Raise BI limits** — the true lawsuit shield.
4. **Add rental + roadside** — small dollars, big bad-day relief.
5. **Last: shuffle deductibles** — only after limits are sane.

## How assets and renters change the answer

- **Renting, minimal savings?** Liability above minimum is still wise (wage
  garnishment follows you), but the risk of being sued long-term for assets you
  don't have is lower. Modest bumps are fine.
- **Own a home, retirement savings, business?** Higher limits matter more,
  because those *are* what a judgment reaches. Ask an agent about adding an
  **umbrella policy** on top — often under $200/year for $1M extra liability.

## State-specific wrinkles (verify with your state)

- **No-fault states** (e.g., FL, MI, NY, NJ, PA, KS, MN, UT, HI, KY, MA, ND, SD,
  PR/territories) use PIP first, and some have wild minimum quirks (Michigan's
  history, Florida's PIP-only minimum).
- **Some states require UM/UIM; some let you sign it away in writing.** Never
  sign it away without pricing what it actually costs.
- **Credit-based scores are banned in California, Hawaii, Massachusetts,
  Michigan (for rating), among others** — so quotes there ignore credit entirely.
- Your state insurance department's website has a plain-English consumer guide
  with the exact current minimums. Spend five minutes there; it beats any blog.

## If you can only afford minimums

Minimum coverage beats no coverage — that is legally true and worth saying.
Fraudulent "cheaper" tags (fake VIN swaps, fronting, phantom drivers) are not
options here; those turn a bad day into a crime. Instead:

- Price a telematics/discount program (chapter 03).
- Take a defensive-driving course — real discount in many states.
- Shift deductible up *only if* you can actually pay it.
- Re-shop every 6 months; the discount ladder is real.
- Ask about low-mileage/pay-per-mile programs if you drive under ~8,000
  miles/year.

One caution: a small handful of states allow "named driver" or stripped-down
policies that are cheap for a reason. Read every exclusion before buying.
