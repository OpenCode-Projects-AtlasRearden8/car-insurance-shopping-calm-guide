# 01 — What the Coverage Words Actually Mean

Insurance paperwork is written so precisely it becomes unreadable. Here is the
vocabulary test, passed. Seven core terms, then the supporting cast.

## The seven core terms

### 1. Liability — pays *other people* when you're at fault

Two halves, printed together like `50/100/50`:

- **Bodily injury per person ($50k)** — max to any one person you hurt.
- **Bodily injury per accident ($100k)** — max for all people in one crash.
- **Property damage ($50k)** — max for cars, fences, storefronts you damage.

Not covered: your own car or your own injuries. Liability exists mainly to
protect the other side — and to protect you from lawsuits up to the limits.

### 2. Collision — your car after a crash

Pays to repair or replace *your* vehicle when it hits (or is hit by) something,
regardless of fault. It carries a **deductible**.

### 3. Comprehensive — your car when it's *not* a crash

Theft, weather, animal strikes, fire, falling objects, broken glass. ("Full
glass" — free windshield repair in many states — is an add-on here.) It carries
a **deductible**.

Those three together are what people mean by **"full coverage."** It is not an
official product — it's collision + comprehensive + liability.

### 4. Deductible — what you pay first

If the claim is $4,000 and your deductible is $1,000, the insurer sends $3,000.
Higher deductible = lower monthly price, but a bigger hit when something happens.
Rule of thumb: never pick a deductible larger than what you could actually pay
tomorrow without borrowing.

### 5. Premium — what you pay to keep the policy alive

Monthly, or every 6–12 months. Quotes should show the full term so you can compare
totals, not teaser monthly numbers.

### 6. Limits — the ceiling on any payout

Every coverage has one. "State minimum liability" is just a very low limit with
a tired haircut.

### 7. Exclusions — what's simply not covered

Read this section even once. Common ones: business deliveries with a personal
policy, rideshare "gap" periods, intentional damage, racing. If you drive for
Uber/DoorDash, chapter 03 has notes for you.

## The supporting cast

- **Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM)** — pays *you* when the at-fault
  driver has none or too little. In some states it's required; in most it's the
  best cheap coverage you can buy.
- **Medical payments (MedPay)** / **PIP (personal injury protection)** — pays
  medical bills for you and your passengers regardless of fault. PIP can also
  cover lost wages. "No-fault" states lean hard on PIP.
- **Rental reimbursement** — a loaner car while yours is in the shop. Caps are
  per day and total ($30/day, $900 max is typical). Cheap, commonly forgotten.
- **Roadside assistance** — towing, lockouts, jump starts. Often cheaper than a
  separate club membership.
- **Gap coverage** — pays the loan balance after a total loss when the car is
  worth less than you owe. Essential in the first ~2 years of a financed car;
  drop it when the loan balance dips below the car's value.
- **Endorsement/rider** — an add-on that modifies the policy (custom parts,
  pet injury, new-car replacement).

## Reading a declarations page without dread

The "dec page" is a one-to-three page summary sent with every renewal:

1. **Named insured & vehicles** — spelling and VINs must match reality.
2. **Coverages with numbers** — that's the whole shopping list.
3. **Premium total** — matches the term length (6 months? 12?).
4. **Discounts listed** — so you can audit them against chapter 03.

Anything that looks wrong (old address, wrong annual miles, a vehicle you sold)
is an easy phone call that usually lowers the price.

## Common misreadings (worth 90 seconds)

- "Full coverage covers everything" — No. It's collision + comprehensive +
  liability, each with own limits, deductibles, and exclusions.
- "State minimum is enough because it's legal" — Legal ≠ sufficient. Chapter 02.
- "The other driver's insurance pays my rental" — Only if you bought rental
  coverage — or they admit fault quickly and their insurer offers one.
- "My rates only care about my driving record" — Also credit-based insurance
  scores (where legal), miles, garaging address, vehicle, and more.
